# Webcam: Ocean of Storms - what is at the site

Research only. Gathered 6 Oct 2026, second pass the same day after reading NASA's Apollo 12 mission page, the NASA gallery and the Apollo 12 Lunar Surface Journal chapters.
This is the source sheet for the HUD labels and spec sheets.
Positions are relative to the lander, Intrepid. North is up. The lander's front (ladder, plus-Z leg) faces west.

## The site

- Lander: 3.0128 S, 23.4219 W (LROC, Wagner et al. 2017 table updated Aug 2026, +/- 2.2 m). Ocean of Storms. On the north-west rim of Surveyor crater.
- Surveyor 3: 3.0162 S, 23.4180 W (same table, +/- 2.6 m). 103 m south and 118 m east of the lander: 157 m, bearing 131, and 10.3 m lower. NASA says the lander came down 538 feet (164 m) from it.
- All positions in metres from the lander are now in `data/positions/positions.csv`, checked against the orthophoto (19 Oct 2026).
- Surveyor stands on the inner slope of Surveyor crater. The crew found the slope "not as steep as we thought".
- The crater group was nicknamed the Snowman. Surveyor crater is the body, Head crater (about 125 m across) is the head.
- Surveyor crater: 200 m across (Preliminary Science Report; press kit "about 650 feet wide"), about 15 m deep. Surveyor sits about 46 m down a 14 degree slope. Matches the orthophoto.

## The instrument package

Settled: LROC measures the central station 126 m from the lander, bearing 316 (north-west): 88 m west, 91 m north. The "150 m west" figure is wrong.

| Object | What it is | Where, from the central station | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Central station | Radio and power hub. 25 kg, 58 cm antenna. | - | LROC |
| SNAP-27 generator | Plutonium-238 heat to electricity. 3.8 kg plutonium, about 1250 W heat, 70-75 W electric at the start, 20 kg, 46 cm long. | 3.4 m west-north-west (10 ft cable) | Mission Report fig 3-6, McInall map |
| Passive seismometer | Listens for moonquakes and impacts | 2.8 m east-south-east (10 ft) | Mission Report fig 3-6, McInall map. LROC table rounds it to 3 m SW, inside its error. |
| Magnetometer | Measures the Moon's magnetic field | 16 m south-south-east (50 ft cable) | Mission Report fig 3-6, journal, McInall map |
| Solar wind spectrometer | Measures particles streaming from the Sun | 4.7 m south (13 ft) | Mission Report fig 3-6, McInall map |
| Ion detector and cold cathode gauge | Measures ions and the thin lunar gas | 18 m west-south-west (60 ft cable; PSR says about 50 ft SW). Gauge 3-5 ft from the detector. | PSR p.85, Mission Report fig 3-6, McInall map |
| Seismometer thermal cover | Thrown aside | 7 m north-east | McInall map |
| Dust detector | Measures dust settling on the hardware | on the central station, I believe | open |

The whole package weighed 250 pounds on Earth, about 40 on the Moon.

## In the crater

| Object | What it is | State | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

| Al Bean's silver pin | His astronaut pin, thrown toward Surveyor from the crater rim | Somewhere on the slope. Nobody knows where. | his own account |
| Close-up stereo camera | The "gold camera" | Left on the surface on the second moonwalk. Exact place open. | journal, weak |

The crew tried to cut off pieces of Surveyor's mirrors and gave up. They had also smuggled a self-timer along for a photo of both of them at Surveyor. They could not find it in the bag. It turned up later and went home.

## Removed from Surveyor 3

TV camera, sampling scoop, a piece of cable, aluminium tubing. About 10 kg.
The camera is in the National Air and Space Museum in Washington. The scoop is at JPL.
The claim that bacteria survived on the camera for two and a half years is disputed. The contamination may have happened after it came home.

## The generator, for the readout

- Plutonium-238 half-life: 87.7 years.
- Fraction of heat left after 57 years: 0.637. About 800 W of the original 1250 W.
- Electric output falls faster than heat because the thermocouples age. After ten years the units still gave more than 90 percent of their starting output. No measurement exists after 1977.
- For the page: show heat, which is physics. Do not show a made-up electric figure.

## What the pass turned up for the picture

- Fallen antenna, standing flag. The opposite of what most people assume.
- The backpacks hit the ground hard enough for the seismometer to record them. That is a moment the time controls can show.
- A roll of undeveloped colour film is still lying there.
- Surveyor is tan, not white.
- Lunar eclipses. When Earth covers the Sun, the camera would see a red ring in the sky and the ground going dark copper. Since everything is computed from the date, these come for free. Not designed in yet.

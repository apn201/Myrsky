The challenge

Since 1967, NASA has left machines on the Moon: landers, rovers, science stations. Kids mostly hear about the missions flying now.

Our idea: let them visit one real place, the Apollo 12 site, and travel through its history by playing with time.

Two NASA machines stand here, 160 m apart: the robot Surveyor 3 (1967) and the lunar module Intrepid (1969), plus a flag, a fallen antenna and a science station that ran for 8 years.

2 / 7   Surveyor 3 alone, April 1967

The experience

  • Opens on LIVE: tonight it is lunar night, lit by Earth.
  • Hold REW: hours, then years fly by. Hardware appears and vanishes at the exact minute it did.
  • Pan, zoom, switch to CAM 2 in Surveyor crater.
  • Click any object for its story, in plain language, with sources.
  • Works on a phone. No install, no login.

Story mode

For school-age visitors: "Tell me the story" plays a 2-minute guided tour with captions.

Night on the Moon today, Earth in the sky, rewind to 1967, the landing with 5-degree shadows, the flag, the science station and its nuclear generator, the visit to Surveyor, liftoff, the 1977 switch-off, back to live.

NASA data makes it possible

  • LROC (LRO): 2 m terrain model and 0.5 m orbital photo of the site; coordinates of every piece of hardware
  • SLDEM2015 (LOLA + JAXA Kaguya): terrain to the horizon
  • NSSDCA, Mission Report, Press Kit, Science Report: sizes, layout, facts
  • Apollo Lunar Surface Journal: every event time
  • NASA 3D Resources: the lunar module

Checks: Sun at touchdown 5.15° (documented 5.1°). Sun direction fitted from the 2010 photo's shading 272°, computed 273°.

What's next

The same idea, "the picture is a function of the time", works anywhere NASA has mapped:

  • All six Apollo sites, Surveyors, Lunokhods
  • Mars: Viking, Pathfinder and Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, InSight, with HiRISE terrain
  • A classroom mode: students check the webcam against the astronauts' own photos
